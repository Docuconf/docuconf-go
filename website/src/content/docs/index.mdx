<Aside type="caution" title="Early days">
docuconf is a young open-source project. The contract specification is a draft (`v1alpha1`), and the CUE schema is prototyped and tested. The language SDKs are being built now. [See the roadmap](roadmap/), and [help shape it](community/).
</Aside>

## The problem

<div class="compare">
<div>

### Today

- Environment variables are undocumented, untyped strings.
- A typo in `DATABSE_URL` is found by a crash loop, often in production.
- Every service invents its own validation, in every language.
- The platform team guesses what each app needs, from READMEs and Slack threads.

</div>
<div>

### With docuconf

- Every variable has a type, constraints and a description, declared in code.
- A bad value is rejected **before a pod starts**, with a precise reason.
- One contract format, whatever the app is written in.
- Platform policy, such as "no debug logging in prod", is layered on top without editing the app.

</div>
</div>

## How it works

<Steps>

1. **Declare** your variables in the env library you already use: caarlos0/env, T3 Env, anyway_config, .NET Options, pydantic-settings. docuconf adds descriptions, secrets and constraints.

2. **Export** a contract. The SDK writes `contract.cue` at build time and publishes it next to your image, tied to its digest.

3. **Validate** before deploy. Your platform (Crossplane and CUE) unifies the contract with the values for this environment and its policy. Anything wrong stops the rollout, with one clear line per problem.

4. **Check again at boot.** The SDK validates the real environment, including secret contents the platform cannot see, and hands your code typed values.

</Steps>

## Principles

<CardGrid>
	<Card title="Your library, not ours" icon="puzzle">
		docuconf extends the leading env library in each language rather than replacing it. Adopting it means adding a package and some metadata, not rewriting your config.
	</Card>
	<Card title="One contract, any language" icon="list-format">
		A Rails app and a .NET service produce the same kind of contract, so the platform validates both the same way.
	</Card>
	<Card title="Fail before deploy" icon="approve-check">
		Missing, mistyped and out-of-range values are caught at composition time, not at 2 a.m. by a crash loop.
	</Card>
	<Card title="Secrets stay secret" icon="warning">
		Contracts never hold secret values. The platform supplies secrets only as references, and errors never print them.
	</Card>
</CardGrid>
